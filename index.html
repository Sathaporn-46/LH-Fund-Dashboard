5. How to Write a Macro Flash Note — Template & Example

A Macro Flash Note is a 1-page investment insight you'll be expected to produce within 24 hours of a major market event. Here is the standard template you should internalize:

▶ Watch first: 🎬 Ray Dalio: How The Economic Machine Works (30 min) ↗ 🎬 Macroeconomics Made Simple ↗ 🎬 Top-Down vs Bottom-Up Investing ↗
🧠 Distilled — Ray Dalio: "How The Economic Machine Works"
The 3 Forces That Drive Every Economy:
1. Productivity Growth — The long-term trend. Comes from innovation and hard work. Slow and steady — not cyclical. This is why GDP per capita rises over centuries.
2. Short-Term Debt Cycle (5–8 years) — Central banks control it. Low rates → credit expands → boom → inflation rises → central bank hikes rates → credit contracts → recession → rates cut → repeat.
3. Long-Term Debt Cycle (75–100 years) — Debt accumulates over decades. Eventually debt service overwhelms income. Ends in "deleveraging" — painful restructuring that takes years.

Credit ≠ Money. Credit is more important. When you borrow to buy something, you create a liability for yourself but income for the seller. This is why credit fuels booms — and why its collapse causes recessions. Most economic "growth" in modern economies is credit-driven.

The Deleveraging Toolkit (when rates hit 0%): Governments have 4 tools: (1) Austerity — cut spending; (2) Debt defaults — write off bad loans; (3) Wealth redistribution — raise taxes on rich; (4) Print money — inflate away debt. A "beautiful deleveraging" uses all four in balance so nominal GDP growth slightly exceeds nominal interest rates.

💡 Dalio's Golden Rule for investors: "Don't get caught up in the moment — know where you are in the cycle." Rate-hike cycle ending? That's the best time to accumulate bonds (LHGDIV, LH Global Income). Deleveraging starting? Accumulate gold (LHGOLD) and defensive quality (LHLONGEVITY).

1. What Is Asset Allocation? (And Why It's Your New Core Skill)

Asset allocation is the decision of how to divide an investment portfolio across different asset classes — equities, bonds, real estate, alternatives, cash — to achieve a desired return with an acceptable level of risk.

Research consistently shows that asset allocation explains ~90% of portfolio return variation over time (Brinson, Hood & Beebower, 1986). Stock-picking and market timing — your previous world — account for the remaining ~10%.

Strategic AA (SAA)
Long-term target allocations based on risk profile. Rebalanced periodically. Example: 60% equity / 30% bonds / 10% alternatives. This is the investor's "anchor" portfolio.
Tactical AA (TAA)
Short-term deviations from SAA based on market conditions (your macro framework from Week 2). Example: Overweight EM equities this quarter because China stimulus is accelerating. This is where your CMT skills add value.

3. Risk-Adjusted Return Metrics — The Language of Portfolio Management

These metrics are the vocabulary of every Investment Committee meeting. You must be fluent in all of them — not just define them, but interpret what they mean for specific LH Fund products.

Risk-Adjusted Metrics 🎬 Sharpe vs. Sortino Explained ↗ 🎬 Analyse Hedge Fund Performance (Sharpe/Sortino/Treynor) ↗ 🎬 Sharpe, Treynor & Alpha (CFA-level) ↗
🧠 Distilled — "Sharpe vs. Sortino Explained" + "Analyse Hedge Fund Performance":
Why Sortino > Sharpe for asymmetric funds: Sharpe penalises ALL volatility equally — upside and downside. But investors don't mind upside volatility! A fund that jumps +30% then consolidates gets punished by Sharpe even though performance was good. Sortino only penalises downside deviation — far better metric for income and defensive funds like LH Global Income and LHGDIV.
The alpha trap: Many funds claim positive alpha, but it disappears after adjusting for factor exposures (value, size, momentum, quality). True alpha — skill above all factors — is rare. When evaluating LH Fund's active managers (GMO, Wellington, T. Rowe Price), always ask: "Is this genuine alpha, or factor beta dressed up as skill?"
Beta intuition for client conversations: A fund with β = 1.3 gains 13% when the market gains 10% — but loses 13% when market loses 10%. Always confirm the client can stomach the downside beta before recommending high-β products like LHSEMICON (β ≈ 1.4) or LHHUMANOID (β ≈ 1.5).
Metric Formula / Definition How to Interpret LH Fund Application
Sharpe Ratio (Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Standard Deviation Higher = better risk-adjusted return. Above 1.0 is good; above 2.0 is excellent. Use when comparing LHGENHANCED vs. pure LHUS — does the 80/20 model add Sharpe vs. plain S&P500?
Sortino Ratio (Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Downside Deviation Like Sharpe but only penalizes downside volatility. Better metric for asymmetric return funds. Use for LHGDIV and LH Global Income — income funds should have high Sortino (limited downside) even if Sharpe is moderate.
Max Drawdown Peak-to-trough decline over any period Critical for risk-averse Thai investors. A fund with -40% max drawdown will terrify conservative clients. LHSEMICON can have -40%+ drawdowns (as in 2022). Always disclose and contextualize — "but the 3-year recovery was +120%."
Alpha (α) Return above benchmark (e.g., vs. MSCI World) Positive alpha = fund manager adding value above passive index. Key selling point for active funds. Use when pitching GMO Quality Fund or Wellington Climate — "this fund generated +X% alpha over MSCI World over 5 years."
Beta (β) Fund sensitivity relative to its benchmark (1.0 = moves in line) Beta > 1 = amplified market moves (more risk/reward). Beta < 1 = defensive. LHSEMICON β ≈ 1.3–1.5 vs. MSCI World (aggressive). LH Global Income β ≈ 0.2–0.3 (defensive). Use in client suitability conversations.
Information Ratio Alpha / Tracking Error Measures consistency of active management. Higher = manager reliably adds alpha without large index deviation. Use when comparing two active managers for the same mandate — e.g., evaluating a potential new partner vs. existing one.

▶ Watch: 🎬 Hidden Risk: Correlation & Diversification Explained ↗ 🎬 Volatility & Correlation: Keys to Diversification ↗
🧠 Distilled — "The Hidden Risk in Your Portfolio: Correlation & Diversification"
The illusion of diversification: Many investors think owning 20 stocks = diversified. But if all 20 are Thai tech companies, correlation ≈ 0.90 — the portfolio behaves like owning 1 stock. True diversification requires assets with genuinely different return drivers.
Crisis correlation spike: In normal markets, correlations behave as expected. In a crash (March 2020, 2008), correlations spike toward 1.0 — everything falls together. The only reliable crisis diversifiers are government bonds, gold, and cash. This is why LHGOLD and LH Global Income have a role even in growth-oriented portfolios.
The 3-step diversification test:
1. What are the return drivers of each fund? (AI capex, EM growth, interest rates, commodity demand)
2. Are those drivers correlated? (AI capex and EM growth both fall in a global recession)
3. Add assets with fundamentally different drivers (LHGOLD = driven by real rates; LH Global Income = driven by credit spreads).
The LH Fund portfolio rule: No more than 2 large positions with the same primary driver. Don't pile into LHAI + LHSEMICON + LHROBOTE — all three are AI-capex-driven and will fall together in a tech drawdown.
💡 Pitch Angle for Multi-Asset: "Instead of choosing between LHSEMICON and LH Global Income, holding both at a 60/40 split historically produces a better Sharpe ratio than either alone — because when one falls, the other is often stable or rising."
